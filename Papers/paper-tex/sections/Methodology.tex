We systematically evaluated four prompting strategies using OpenAI GPT-4.1-mini across 25 educational paragraphs, conducting 300 total experiments (4 strategies × 25 paragraphs × 3 repetitions).

\subsection{Educational Text Corpus}
Our corpus comprised 25 carefully curated educational paragraphs (200-250 tokens each) containing clear gendered language patterns. Content was sourced from established open educational resources: OpenStax textbooks \cite{openstax2021subjects} (80\%), BCcampus OpenEd (10\%), and MIT OpenCourseWare (10\%). The corpus maintained balanced disciplinary representation (50\% STEM, 50\% humanities) with 1-12 gendered terms per paragraph.

\subsection{Prompting Strategies}
We designed four strategies representing increasing complexity in bias mitigation:

\textbf{Raw Prompt (Control):} Basic rewriting instruction without bias mitigation guidance.

\textbf{System Prompt:} Explicit role assignment as "inclusive writing assistant" with direct bias mitigation instructions.

\textbf{Few-Shot:} Incorporated three concrete examples of gender-neutral transformations demonstrating desired output patterns.

\textbf{Few-Shot + Verification:} Enhanced few-shot approach with explicit self-verification mechanism requiring the model to review and confirm gender neutrality.

Complete prompt templates are provided in Appendix A.

\subsection{Evaluation Framework}
We employed comprehensive evaluation encompassing three dimensions:

\textbf{Gender Bias Reduction:} Automated detection using regex patterns for gendered terms \cite{ningrum2020developing}, calculating percentage reduction from original text.

\textbf{Semantic Preservation:} BLEU-4 scores measuring similarity between original and transformed text.

\textbf{Consistency Analysis:} Variance assessment across experimental repetitions to evaluate reliability using ANOVA statistical methods \cite{dror2018anova}.
